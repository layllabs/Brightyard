Landscape maintenance for apartment buildings: a property manager's guide

A practical checklist for multifamily properties in the San Fernando Valley and Ventura County: what to look at on every visit, irrigation and water bills, turnovers and bulky items, hillside fire rules, the leaf blower ban, organic waste rules for buildings with five or more units, and how Brightyard works with property managers.

By Joaquin, owner of Brightyard · Last reviewed November 19, 2026

The short version

  • Walkways come first. Overgrown shrubs, lifted pavers, and roots across paths are what residents trip on and complain about.
  • Leaks show up on the water bill before anyone sees them. Have every irrigation zone run and walked regularly. LADWP requires leaks to be fixed promptly.
  • Turnovers go faster with a plan. LA Sanitation picks up bulky items from buildings with five or more units by appointment, and anything a former tenant left needs California's notice process first.
  • Gas leaf blowers are banned within 500 feet of a home in the City of Los Angeles, and the rule covers whoever hired the crew as well as the operator.
  • Buildings with five or more units have organic waste duties. Under SB 1383, they need organics collection or self-hauling, and landscape waste counts.
  • Hillside buildings may need brush clearance. In the City of LA's Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones, clearance is a year-round responsibility.
  • The state turf ban doesn't apply to residential properties, but condo and HOA common areas are covered from January 1, 2029.

Every visit

The visit checklist for multifamily properties

Apartment landscaping gets used hard and noticed fast. A routine visit should cover:

  • Walkways and stairs: shrubs and groundcover trimmed back, debris cleared, and trip hazards like lifted edging or exposed roots reported.
  • Windows and lights: hedges kept below window sills and off exterior lights, so units get light and paths stay lit at night.
  • Entries and courtyards: the first thing a prospective tenant sees on a tour.
  • Trash enclosures and parking areas: litter and windblown debris picked up from the planting around them.
  • Lawns and beds: mowing, edging, and weeding.
  • Irrigation: a look for wet spots, dry plants, and water running onto pavement.
  • Drains: yard drains and inlets kept clear, especially from November through March, when nearly all of the Valley's rain falls.

It also helps to get a short note after each visit with anything that needs a decision, such as a dying tree, a broken valve, or a tenant's belongings left outside.

Water

Irrigation and water bills

On a multifamily property, a broken head or leaking valve can run for weeks before anyone notices, and the owner pays for it. The reliable way to catch problems is to run each zone and walk it.

If your building is served by LADWP

  • Sprinklers run on assigned days: odd-numbered addresses on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, and even-numbered addresses on Sunday, Tuesday, and Thursday.
  • No outdoor watering from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Standard spray nozzles get one cycle of up to 8 minutes per station per watering day. Water-conserving nozzles get two cycles of up to 15 minutes.
  • No runoff, no watering hard surfaces, and leaks have to be fixed promptly.

Narrow strips along buildings and parkways are hard to water with spray heads without hitting walls and sidewalks. Our sprinkler guide covers when drip makes more sense, plus rebates.

Between tenants

Turnovers and bulky items

A unit turnover often comes with a patio or yard to clear. A few rules to know first:

  • Bulky item pickup: LA Sanitation picks up large household items from multifamily buildings with five or more units. Call 1-800-773-2489 or use the MyLA311 app to make an appointment, and have an itemized list ready. Don't set items out without an appointment.
  • Belongings a tenant left behind: before anything is hauled away, follow California's notice rules for property left after a tenancy ends (Civil Code sections 1980 to 1991). This is general information, not legal advice.
  • Paint and chemicals: don't go to the curb or in the dumpster.

Our bulky item guide has the details, including what the city won't take and where paint and chemicals go.

Noise and equipment

Leaf blowers and work hours

In the City of Los Angeles, Municipal Code Section 112.04 bans gas-powered blowers within 500 feet of a residence at any time. The ban applies to both the person using the blower and the person who hired them. The same section bars lawn mowers, backpack blowers, edgers, and other loud equipment between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. within 500 feet of a residence.

An apartment building is a residence, so inside the city, gas blowers are effectively off the table on site. Other cities set their own rules. Either way, put the allowed work hours in the agreement.

SB 1383

Organic waste rules for buildings with five or more units

Under California's organic waste law, SB 1383, multifamily complexes with five or more units have to either join their local organics collection program or self-haul organic waste to an approved facility. The owner or manager has to provide organics collection for tenants and employees, supply properly labeled containers, educate tenants every year, and give new tenants information within 14 days of moving in.

CalRecycle lists landscape and pruning waste as organic waste. A landscaper who hauls clippings away counts as a self-hauler, which in Calabasas, for example, means taking it to an organics facility or composter and keeping delivery records for five years. Ask your landscaper where the green waste goes, and keep bags of clippings out of the trash dumpsters.

Fire season

Hillside buildings and brush clearance

Many of the Valley's hillside neighborhoods fall in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone. In the City of Los Angeles, the fire department treats brush clearance as a year-round responsibility. The main requirements:

  • Cut grass and reduce native brush to about 3 inches within 200 feet of structures, with exceptions for well-spaced individual native shrubs.
  • Keep 10 feet clear along combustible fences and driveways, and 10 feet between foliage and any chimney outlet.
  • On trees taller than 18 feet, remove lower branches so no foliage is within 6 feet of the ground.
  • Keep 5 feet between the roof and any overhanging branches, and keep roofs and gutters free of leaves and debris.

You can look up a parcel's requirements on the LAFD's Fire Inspection Management System (FIMS). Los Angeles County and Ventura County fire departments set their own rules outside the city. Our plant care guide covers hillside upkeep in more detail.

Know before you cut

Street trees and protected trees

Parkway trees in Los Angeles are city street trees. A no-fee permit from the StreetsLA Urban Forestry Division is required to prune one. The city doesn't prune roots, but property owners can do required root pruning at their own expense with a no-fee permit. Call Urban Forestry at (213) 847-3077 if you're not sure whether a tree is a street tree.

On private property, the City of Los Angeles protects most native oaks (scrub oak is the exception), western sycamore, Southern California black walnut, and California bay, along with toyon and Mexican elderberry, once they measure at least 4 inches in diameter at 4½ feet above the ground. Removing or relocating one takes a city permit.

AB 1572

Does the state's turf ban apply to apartment buildings?

The State Water Resources Control Board says its nonfunctional turf ban doesn't apply to residential properties. Its FAQ doesn't name apartment buildings specifically, so check with your water provider about a particular property.

Condo associations and other common interest developments are different. Their common areas are covered starting January 1, 2029, and our HOA maintenance guide explains what that means.

FAQ

Common questions

What should apartment landscape maintenance include?

Routine visits should keep walkways and stairs clear, hedges off windows and exterior lights, entries and courtyards tidy, litter picked up around trash enclosures and parking, lawns and beds mowed and weeded, drains clear, and irrigation checked for leaks and runoff. A short note after each visit helps flag anything that needs a decision.

How can I lower the water bill at an apartment building?

Have every irrigation zone run and walked regularly to catch broken heads, leaking valves, and runoff, and adjust controllers as the seasons change. LADWP limits watering to assigned days, bans watering from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., and requires leaks to be fixed promptly.

Does LA pick up bulky items from apartment buildings?

Yes. LA Sanitation picks up large household items from multifamily buildings with five or more units. Call 1-800-773-2489 or use the MyLA311 app to make an appointment, and have an itemized list ready.

What do I do with belongings a former tenant left outside?

Before anything is hauled away, follow California's notice rules for property left after a tenancy ends, in Civil Code sections 1980 to 1991. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do apartment buildings have to recycle green waste?

Under SB 1383, multifamily complexes with five or more units have to join organics collection or self-haul organic waste to an approved facility, and landscape and pruning waste counts. Owners and managers also have to provide containers and educate tenants every year.

Can landscapers use gas leaf blowers at apartment buildings in Los Angeles?

Not in the City of Los Angeles. Municipal Code Section 112.04 bans gas-powered blowers within 500 feet of a residence at any time, and it applies to both the person using the blower and the person who hired them.

Does the nonfunctional turf ban apply to apartment buildings?

The State Water Resources Control Board says the ban doesn't apply to residential properties, though its FAQ doesn't name apartment buildings specifically, so check with your water provider. Common areas of condo associations and other common interest developments are covered starting January 1, 2029.
