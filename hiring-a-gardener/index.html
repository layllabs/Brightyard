The basics

What should a gardener do on every visit?

  • Mow and edge the lawn, if you have one.
  • Weed the beds, pulling or hoeing weeds while they're small, before they flower and seed.
  • Trim what's crowding walkways, windows, the driveway, and the house.
  • Clean up the patio, walkways, and driveway after the work.
  • Deal with the clippings: into your green bin the right way, or hauled away. Not into the street. Leaves and clippings that reach a storm drain can clog it during a storm, and storm drains flow untreated to the ocean.
  • Look at the sprinklers: wet spots, dry plants, broken heads, and water running onto pavement.
  • Tell you what needs a decision: a dying shrub, pests, a leaning tree, or a broken valve.

Beyond the weekly visit

What should happen monthly and by season?

  • Run and walk every sprinkler zone regularly, and again after storms or wind, which break heads and knock drip lines loose.
  • Adjust the controller as the weather changes, and check it after a power outage, which can reset its clock. A summer schedule left running all winter wastes water.
  • Shape hedges and shrubs a few times a year, timed to the plants.
  • Clear drains and gutters before storms. Nearly all of the Valley's rain falls between November and March.
  • Stay ahead of winter weeds. They sprout in the weeks after the first soaking rain, and some set seed about six weeks after they sprout.
  • Clean up after Santa Ana winds, which can blow any time from September through May.
  • Watch watering through summer. July through September is the stress season for Valley plants.

Know the rules

Does a gardener need a license in California?

Contractor's license

The Contractors State License Board (CSLB) says gardeners who maintain landscaping and are paid on a regular basis generally don't need a C-27 landscaping license. Bigger work is different. CSLB says a project needs a licensed contractor once it reaches $1,000 in combined labor and materials, needs a permit, or involves hired workers. It also lists tree pruning above 15 feet, tree removal, stump removal, and irrigation systems as landscaping work that needs a license. You can look up any contractor's license on cslb.ca.gov.

Pesticide license

Under California law, a gardener who applies pesticides, even occasionally as part of maintenance work, needs a Maintenance Gardener Pest Control Business License from the Department of Pesticide Regulation (DPR) and has to register with the county agricultural commissioner. Someone at the business also needs a DPR applicator certificate. Weed killers count as pesticides. A gardener who doesn't spray anything doesn't need this license.

Noise and equipment

Leaf blowers and work hours

In the City of Los Angeles, Municipal Code Section 112.04 bans gas-powered blowers within 500 feet of a residence at any time. The ban applies to both the person using the blower and the person who hired them, which means the homeowner too. The same section bars lawn mowers, backpack blowers, edgers, and other loud equipment between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. within 500 feet of a residence.

Other cities in the Valley and Ventura County set their own rules, so check yours.

Before you hire

Questions to ask before you hire a gardener

  • What exactly happens on each visit, and how often will you come?
  • Do you check the sprinklers, and which repairs are included?
  • Where do the clippings go?
  • Do you use any weed killers or pesticides? If so, do you have a DPR license?
  • What equipment do you use, and does it follow the local leaf blower rules?
  • How do you quote and get approval for extra work?
  • Who will actually show up, and how do I reach you?
  • Will you put the scope and price in writing, and how much notice do you need if I want to stop?
Worth watching

Signs it isn't working

  • Weeds keep going to seed in the beds.
  • Leaves and clippings get blown into the street or gutter.
  • Broken sprinkler heads or soggy spots go unmentioned for weeks.
  • Someone sprays weed killer but can't show a DPR license.
  • Visits happen on no predictable schedule, or you can't reach anyone when something's wrong.
  • Your water bill jumps and nobody has looked at the controller.
FAQ

Common questions

What does a maintenance gardener do?

On a typical visit: mowing and edging, weeding beds, trimming plants back from walkways and windows, cleaning up hard surfaces, putting clippings in the green bin or hauling them away, and checking the sprinklers. A good gardener also tells you about problems, like a dying shrub, pests, or a broken valve.

How often should a gardener come?

Common schedules are weekly, every other week, or monthly. Yards with lawns and fast-growing hedges usually need more frequent visits in spring and summer, and growth slows in winter.

Does a gardener need a license in California?

For regular maintenance, generally no. The Contractors State License Board says gardeners who maintain landscaping and are paid on a regular basis generally don't need a C-27 license. A project needs a licensed contractor once it reaches $1,000 in labor and materials, needs a permit, or involves hired workers, and tree pruning above 15 feet needs a license.

Can my gardener spray weed killer?

Only with a license. California requires a gardener who applies any pesticide, including weed killers, to hold a Maintenance Gardener Pest Control Business License from the Department of Pesticide Regulation and to register with the county agricultural commissioner. Ask to see it.

Can my gardener use a gas leaf blower in Los Angeles?

Not in the City of Los Angeles. Municipal Code Section 112.04 bans gas-powered blowers within 500 feet of a residence at any time, which rules them out at any home in the city. The ban applies to both the person using the blower and the person who hired them, so the homeowner can be responsible too.

Where should my gardener put the clippings?

In your green bin, following your city's rules, or hauled away to be recycled. Clippings and leaves shouldn't be blown into the street or gutter, where they can clog storm drains.

What should I ask before hiring a gardener?

Ask what happens on each visit and how often, whether sprinkler checks are included, where the clippings go, whether they use pesticides and have a DPR license, how extra work is quoted, who will show up, and whether they'll put the scope, price, and notice period in writing.
