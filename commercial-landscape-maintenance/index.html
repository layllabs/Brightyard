The routine

What should commercial landscape maintenance include?

For a storefront or small commercial property, a routine visit usually covers:

  • Litter pickup in beds, planters, and parking lot islands.
  • Weeding, including cracks along walkways and curbs.
  • Trimming hedges, shrubs, and groundcover back from walkways, curbs, and parking spaces.
  • Keeping windows, signs, address numbers, and lights clear.
  • Mowing and edging any lawn areas.
  • Cleaning up walkways and entries after the work.
  • A look at the irrigation: wet spots, dry plants, and water running onto pavement.

Seasonal work comes on top of that: leaf drop, clearing drains before storms, cleanup after wind events, and resetting irrigation as the weather changes. Nearly all of the Valley's rain falls between November and March, and our heavy rain guide covers what to check before a storm.

Visibility

Windows, signs, and sight lines

The Los Angeles Police Department promotes Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED), which lists landscaping, fencing, and lighting among its design recommendations. One of its core ideas is natural surveillance: people should be able to see what's happening around a building, from inside and out.

City crime prevention programs often turn that into a simple rule for planting near buildings. The City of Tustin's CPTED guidance recommends keeping shrubs trimmed to 3 feet and pruning the lower branches of trees up to 6 feet. The City of Roseville's suggests hedges no higher than 3 feet and tree canopies starting no lower than 8 feet, especially around entries and windows.

  • Keep shrubs below window sills and off the glass.
  • Raise tree canopies so they don't block signs, lights, or the view of the entrance.
  • Trim back anything that crowds a walkway, a parking space, or a building corner.

The same trimming keeps your signage visible from the street, which matters to any business that depends on walk-in customers.

Cannabis retail

Landscaping at cannabis dispensaries

Licensed cannabis businesses in California have to keep a video surveillance system, and state regulations (California Code of Regulations, Title 4, section 15044) require entrances and exits to be recorded from both indoor and outdoor vantage points. A shrub or tree that grows into an exterior camera's view is a compliance problem, not just a looks problem.

In the City of Los Angeles, a storefront retailer's license also has to be displayed so it's readable from outside the business. Keep planters and hedges from covering it.

  • Walk the exterior cameras' views during each visit and trim anything creeping in.
  • Keep growth off windows and the posted license.
  • Schedule work at a time that suits your security staff and opening hours.
Food service

Restaurants and patios

For restaurants, timing matters as much as the work. Blowing, edging, and trimming kick up dust and debris, so they belong before opening or between services, never while guests are seated outside.

  • Keep planters and hedges trimmed back from patio seating and walkways.
  • Clear fallen leaves and flowers from patios and entries on each visit.
  • Check that irrigation doesn't spray onto seating, walkways, or the building.
Noise rules

Work hours and leaf blowers

Many businesses want landscaping done before they open. In the City of Los Angeles, Municipal Code Section 112.04 limits how early that can be when homes are nearby:

  • Between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., no one may run lawn mowers, backpack blowers, edgers, or other loud equipment within 500 feet of a residence.
  • Gas-powered blowers are banned within 500 feet of a residence at any time.
  • The blower ban applies to both the person using the blower and the person who hired them.

Many Valley storefronts and strip centers sit within 500 feet of homes, so check where yours is. Other cities set their own rules.

The public right-of-way

Street trees and parkways

In Los Angeles, trees in the parkway between the sidewalk and the street are city street trees, managed by the StreetsLA Urban Forestry Division. A no-fee permit is required to prune one, and pruning has to follow International Society of Arboriculture guidelines. The city doesn't prune roots, but property owners can do required root pruning at their own expense with a no-fee permit from Urban Forestry.

If you're not sure whether a tree is a street tree, call Urban Forestry at (213) 847-3077. Our fallen city tree guide covers what to do if one comes down.

Coming in 2028

The January 1, 2028 turf deadline for commercial properties

A state law, AB 1572, phases out using drinking water on nonfunctional turf. Commercial, industrial, and institutional properties have to comply starting January 1, 2028.

The State Water Resources Control Board defines functional turf as mowed grass in a recreational use area or community space. Mowed grass anywhere else is nonfunctional, and the board specifically names grass in street rights-of-way and parking lots. That covers a lot of decorative commercial lawn.

  • The law limits watering decorative grass with drinking water. It doesn't require removing the grass.
  • Trees and other perennial plantings that aren't grass can still be watered with drinking water.
  • Properties with more than 5,000 square feet of irrigated area have to self-certify compliance, first by June 30, 2030, then every three years until 2039.

Converting lawn to drip-irrigated planting is installation work for a licensed landscape contractor. Check your water provider's rebates before you start, and ask them if you're unsure whether an area counts as nonfunctional.

Where it goes

Green waste and California's organic waste law

Under SB 1383, businesses have to either use organics collection or self-haul organic waste to an approved facility, and CalRecycle lists landscape and pruning waste as organic waste. A landscaper who hauls clippings off your property counts as a self-hauler, which in Calabasas, for example, means taking it to an organics facility or composter and keeping delivery records for five years.

Ask your landscaper where the green waste goes, and keep bags of clippings out of your trash bins.

FAQ

Common questions

What does commercial landscape maintenance include?

For a storefront or small commercial property, routine visits usually cover litter pickup in beds and planters, weeding, trimming plants back from walkways and parking, keeping windows, signs, and lights clear, mowing any lawn, cleaning up walkways and entries, and checking the irrigation. Seasonal cleanups and larger projects are usually quoted separately.

How tall should shrubs be in front of a business?

Crime prevention guidance from California cities, based on Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design, commonly recommends keeping shrubs near windows, doors, and walkways at about 3 feet and raising tree canopies to 6 to 8 feet, so people can see the entrance and windows clearly.

Are there landscaping rules for cannabis dispensaries?

There isn't a landscaping rule as such, but state regulations require licensed cannabis businesses to record entrances and exits from both indoor and outdoor vantage points, so plants can't block exterior cameras. In the City of Los Angeles, a storefront retailer's license also has to be readable from outside.

What time can landscapers start work in Los Angeles?

In the City of Los Angeles, Municipal Code Section 112.04 bars lawn mowers, blowers, edgers, and other loud equipment between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. within 500 feet of a residence. Gas-powered blowers are banned within 500 feet of a residence at any time.

Does the nonfunctional turf ban apply to my business?

Most likely, if you have decorative grass. Starting January 1, 2028, commercial, industrial, and institutional properties can't water nonfunctional turf with drinking water. The State Water Board specifically names grass in parking lots and street rights-of-way as nonfunctional. Trees and other plants can still be watered, and the grass doesn't have to be removed.

Can I trim the street tree in front of my store?

In Los Angeles, parkway trees are city street trees, and a no-fee permit from the StreetsLA Urban Forestry Division is required to prune one. Pruning has to follow International Society of Arboriculture guidelines. Call Urban Forestry at (213) 847-3077 if you're not sure whether a tree is a street tree.

Who handles green waste from a commercial property?

Under California's organic waste law, SB 1383, businesses have to use organics collection or self-haul organic waste to an approved facility, and landscape waste counts. If your landscaper hauls it away, they're a self-hauler and should take it to an organics facility or composter.
